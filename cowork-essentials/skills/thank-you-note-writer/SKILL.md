# Thank You Note Writer

Write a genuine, specific thank you note that actually means something. Not a generic "thanks for everything" but a message that makes the recipient feel seen and valued.

## Instructions

### Step 1: Understand the Situation

From the user's request, identify:

- **Who** — Who are they thanking?
- **What for** — What specifically are they grateful for?
- **Relationship** — Boss, client, mentor, friend, family, colleague, stranger?
- **Occasion** — Gift, help, referral, interview, hospitality, mentorship, going above and beyond?
- **Channel** — Handwritten card, email, text, LinkedIn message, Slack?
- **Tone** — Professional, warm, casual, heartfelt?

### Step 2: Write the Note

The formula: Specific + Impact + Forward

```
[Greeting]

[What you're thanking them for — be SPECIFIC. Not "thanks for your help" but "thanks for staying late on Thursday to walk me through the reporting dashboard."]

[The impact — what their action meant to you or how it helped. This is what makes it genuine instead of obligatory.]

[Forward-looking — what happens next, or how this strengthens the relationship. Optional but powerful.]

[Warm close]
[Your name]
```

### Step 3: Generate Three Versions

**Version 1: Short & Sweet** (2-3 sentences)
Best for: text messages, Slack, quick follow-ups

**Version 2: Standard** (1 short paragraph)
Best for: email, LinkedIn, most professional situations

**Version 3: Heartfelt** (2-3 short paragraphs)
Best for: handwritten cards, mentors, major gestures, personal relationships

### Step 4: Occasion-Specific Guidelines

| Occasion | Key Element |
|----------|-------------|
| **After a job interview** | Thank them for their time, reference something specific from the conversation, restate your enthusiasm |
| **Gift received** | Name the specific gift, say how you'll use it or why it's meaningful |
| **Client referral** | Acknowledge the trust it takes to refer someone, mention the outcome if you know it |
| **Help or favor** | Describe what you were struggling with and how their help changed things |
| **Hospitality** | Mention a specific moment or detail from the visit that stood out |
| **Mentorship** | Name the specific advice or lesson and how you've applied it |
| **Going above and beyond** | Acknowledge that they didn't have to do this, and that you noticed |
| **Business partnership** | Highlight a specific win from working together |

### Step 5: Deliver

Present all three versions. Let the user pick and customize.

Offer: "Want me to adjust the tone, add a specific detail, or write a follow-up for later?"

## Rules

- Specificity is everything. "Thanks for dinner" is forgettable. "Thanks for making that incredible risotto and for the conversation about starting a business — I haven't stopped thinking about your advice on pricing" is not.
- Never be generic. If you can swap out the recipient's name and the note still works, it's not specific enough.
- Keep it real. Don't overdo the flattery. Genuine beats gushing.
- Match the channel. A text should be 2-3 sentences. A handwritten card can be longer. An email sits in between.
- Timing matters. Mention that the best thank you notes are sent within 24-48 hours. If it's been longer, don't mention the delay, just send it.
