# Case Study Writer

Turn a client project or result into a compelling case study that sells your work. Give it the details and get a polished, ready-to-publish document that follows the proven Problem > Solution > Result framework.

## Instructions

### Step 1: Gather the Story

From the user's request, identify:

- **Client name** — Who was the client? (Can be anonymized if needed)
- **Industry** — What space are they in?
- **The problem** — What was the client struggling with before?
- **What you did** — What services or solutions did you provide?
- **The results** — What changed? Numbers are gold. Before/after is gold.
- **Timeline** — How long did the engagement take?
- **Quote** — Any direct quote from the client? If not, skip it.

If the user gives a loose description like "I helped a client double their leads," ask one focused question to get the specifics. You need the before, the what, and the after.

### Step 2: Write the Case Study

Follow this structure:

```markdown
# [Compelling Title — Result-Focused]

**Client:** [Name or "A [Industry] Company"]
**Industry:** [Industry]
**Timeline:** [Duration]
**Services:** [What you provided]

---

## The Challenge

[2-3 paragraphs. Paint the picture of where the client was before. What was broken, frustrating, or costing them money? Use specific details. Make the reader think "that sounds like me."]

## The Approach

[2-3 paragraphs. What did you actually do? Walk through your process in plain English. Don't list features — tell the story of how you solved the problem. Include any key decisions or pivots.]

## The Results

[Lead with the headline number or transformation. Then break down the specific outcomes.]

**Key Results:**
- [Result 1 — with a number if possible]
- [Result 2]
- [Result 3]

[1 paragraph of context around the results. What do these numbers mean for their business?]

## Client Testimonial

> "[Direct quote from the client, if available]"
> — [Client Name], [Title] at [Company]

[If no quote is available, skip this section entirely. Never fabricate quotes.]

## Key Takeaway

[1-2 sentences. What's the lesson? What should a prospect reading this take away about working with you?]
```

### Step 3: Title Rules

The title should lead with the result, not the process:

**Good:**
- "How [Client] Increased Revenue 3x in 90 Days"
- "From 2 Leads a Month to 47: A [Industry] Transformation"
- "Cutting Onboarding Time by 60% for a Growing SaaS Team"

**Bad:**
- "Case Study: [Client Name]"
- "Our Work With [Company]"
- "Website Redesign Project"

### Step 4: Writing Rules

- **Show, don't tell.** "Revenue increased 142%" hits harder than "revenue improved significantly."
- **Numbers are everything.** Before/after metrics, percentages, timelines, dollar amounts. If the user has them, use them prominently.
- **Write for the prospect, not the client.** The reader should see themselves in the challenge section.
- **Never fabricate results or statistics.** If the user gives vague results ("they were really happy"), write around it. Don't invent percentages.
- **Keep it under 800 words.** Case studies that drag lose readers.

### Step 5: Save and Deliver

Save as: `Case Study - [Client or Project Name].md`

Offer: "Want me to make a shorter version for your website, or turn this into a LinkedIn post?"

## Rules

- Never invent metrics, quotes, or results. If the user doesn't have numbers, write a narrative-style case study focused on the transformation.
- If the client needs to be anonymous, use "[Industry] Company" and strip identifying details.
- Always include the Key Takeaway section. It's the sales closer.
